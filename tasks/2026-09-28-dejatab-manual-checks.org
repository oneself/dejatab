* What this is

Everything in DejaTab that only Chrome can settle. The automated suite covers
the URL rules and the settings logic; nothing in it can prove that a banner
renders on a real site, that Chrome refuses to inject into the Web Store, or
that the extension is absent from the task manager while the user reads.

Each item says what to set up, what to do, what you should see, and which
requirement or task it proves. Work through them in order: the later ones
assume the earlier ones passed.

These items close sub-tasks 1.7, 5.2 and 5.8, and they are the content of
7.7, 7.8 and 7.9 in [[file:2026-09-27-dejatab-tasks.org][the task list]].

* Setup, once

1. Open ~chrome://extensions~ and turn on Developer mode, top right.
2. Click "Load unpacked" and pick the repository root, the directory holding
   ~manifest.json~.
3. After editing any file, press the reload arrow on the DejaTab card. After
   editing ~manifest.json~, remove the extension and load it again.
4. Open DejaTab's settings by clicking its toolbar icon.

Unless an item says otherwise, leave the rules at their shipped values and
both lists empty. The quickest way back to that state is "Reset to defaults"
at the bottom of the settings page.

* Install and permissions

** 1. The extension loads clean

On ~chrome://extensions~ the DejaTab card shows no "Errors" button, and
clicking "Service worker" opens a console with no red entries. Click the
toolbar icon: the settings page opens in a tab and every section renders.

Proves task 1.7 and FR-22.

** 2. The permission warning is the one the PRD expects

On the card, open "Details" and read the site-access text. It must say
DejaTab can read and change your data on all sites, and nothing about
browsing history, downloads or anything else.

Proves task 1.7, and the FR-13 privacy story the store listing repeats.

* The four answers

** 3. A tracking parameter does not earn a second tab

Open an article. In a new tab open the same article with ~?utm_source=test~
appended. A dark bar appears at the top of the second tab reading "DejaTab
found 1 other tab on this page. Close it?", with Yes, No, Always and Never,
the shortened address beside it, and Yes already focused. Click Yes: the
first tab closes, the bar disappears, and a Chrome notification says it
closed 1 duplicate tab and names the page.

Proves FR-6, FR-7, FR-12 and R5.

** 4. No keeps everything

Repeat 3 and click No. Both tabs stay open, the bar disappears, no setting
changes. Reload the second tab: the bar appears again.

Proves FR-7.

** 5. Dismissing without choosing means No

Repeat 3 and press Escape. The bar disappears and nothing closes.

Proves FR-9's dismissal clause.

** 6. Always stops the asking

Repeat 3 and click Always. The duplicate closes and the notification names
it. In settings, Per-site rules now holds an entry for that host with "Close
duplicates without asking" ticked, labelled "set by Always" with today's
date. Make another duplicate on that host: no bar at all, the duplicate
closes by itself, and only the notification tells you.

Proves task 5.8, FR-7, FR-8 and FR-12.

** 7. Never makes the site inert

On a different host, trigger the bar and click Never. Nothing closes. In
settings, Excluded sites lists that host as "added by Never" with today's
date. Make two more duplicates there: no bar, no closing, nothing at all.

Proves FR-7, FR-8 and FR-15.

** 8. Never disarms a standing Always

Take the host from 6, which has Always set. Trigger a duplicate on a
subdomain of it and answer Never there. The per-site entry for the parent
host keeps its rule overrides, its origin label and its date, but "Close
duplicates without asking" is now unticked.

Proves FR-8's clause that a Never leaves no armed Always behind.

** 9. Removing an exclusion asks again rather than resuming silently

Remove the Never entry from 7 with its Remove link, then make a duplicate on
that host. The bar returns, because the exclusion is gone and no Always was
left armed.

Proves FR-8 and FR-21.

* What must never be closed

** 10. A pinned tab survives

Open a page, right-click its tab, Pin. Open the same page in a new tab. No
bar appears, because a pinned tab is not a candidate and nothing else
matches.

Proves FR-4.

** 11. A loading tab survives

Open a very slow page and, while it is still loading, open the same address
in another tab. Nothing closes.

Proves FR-4.

** 12. A tab pinned mid-close survives, and the count tells the truth

Open the same page in four tabs. In the fourth, wait for the bar, click Yes,
and immediately pin one of the other three. The pinned tab is still open
afterwards, and the notification names 2 rather than 3.

Proves FR-4 and FR-11 together with the close-time re-check.

** 13. A tab that moved on survives

Open the same page in three tabs. In the third, wait for the bar, switch to
the first tab and navigate it somewhere else, come back and click Yes. Only
the tab still showing the page closes, and the notification says 1.

Proves that a plan made before the answer can never cost a tab the user has
since moved.

** 14. Internal pages are untouched

Open ~chrome://settings~ in two tabs, then ~chrome://extensions~ in two
more. No bar anywhere, nothing closes.

Proves FR-5.

** 15. Pages Chrome refuses to inject into close nothing

Open the same Chrome Web Store page in two tabs. Repeat with a PDF in
Chrome's own viewer, and with a ~file://~ page. In all three: no bar, and
nothing closes. A question that could not be asked must never become a
close.

Proves task 5.2 and the TSD's Error Handling row for injection failure.

** 16. An excluded site stays excluded when spelled with a trailing dot

Add a host you can navigate to Excluded sites. Open it normally in one tab.
In a second tab navigate to the fully qualified spelling, with a dot after
the last label: ~https://example.com./~ rather than ~https://example.com/~.
No bar, nothing closes.

This is the shape of a real bug. The matcher already stripped that dot when
it built the key, so the two spellings matched as one page, while the
exclusion lookup kept the dot and missed its own entry: a prompt and a close
on a site the user had told DejaTab to ignore.

Proves FR-15 and FR-16 against the fully qualified spelling.

** 17. An upper-case host resolves the same entries as its plain form

Add a per-site entry for a host, then navigate to that host with some
letters capitalised. The entry's rules must apply, and if its
close-without-asking box is ticked the duplicate must close with no prompt.

Proves FR-19 and FR-20, and that the worker and the settings page normalise
a host the same way.

** 18. A raw XML page closes nothing

Open a URL serving ~text/xml~ with no stylesheet, in two tabs. No bar,
nothing closes: the banner cannot render in such a document, so the answer
is a silent No.

Proves the same Error Handling rule as 15.

* Across windows

** 19. Another window's duplicates are found

Open a page, move its tab to a new window, then open the same page in the
original window. The bar appears, and Yes closes the tab in the other
window.

Proves FR-3.

* The banner on hostile and awkward pages

** 20. A transformed body does not break it

Find a site whose body gets a transform or a filter: a slide-in drawer menu
and a blur-behind-modal both do this. Open the drawer or the modal, then
trigger a duplicate on that site. The bar must still appear and Yes must
still close the duplicates. The bar may sit slightly inside the page's own
box rather than flush to the window edge, which is expected and correct.

This is the case a strict geometry check broke, turning every answer into a
silent No. It is the most important item in this list.

** 21. A filter or contain on the root element

As 20, but with the property on ~html~ rather than ~body~, including a page
in fullscreen. Yes must still close.

** 22. The bar is legible and on top anywhere

Trigger it on a light site, a dark site, a site with its own fixed cookie
banner, and a site with a full-screen overlay. DejaTab's bar sits above
everything, with its own dark background and light text, nothing of the page
bleeding through and nothing of the page restyled.

Proves tasks 5.3 and 5.4.

** 23. No site can style, read or click it

With the bar up, open DevTools and evaluate
~document.getElementById("dejatab-banner-host").shadowRoot~. It must return
null, because the root is closed.

Proves task 5.3 and the TSD's Security section.

* The timeout

** 24. It gives up on its own and closes nothing

Leave the timeout at its shipped 30 seconds. Trigger the bar and do not
touch it. After 30 seconds it fades out and vanishes, and nothing closes.
Reload that tab: the bar returns.

Proves FR-9.

** 25. Hovering holds it open

Trigger the bar, then keep the pointer over it for a minute: it stays. Move
the pointer away and time it: it fades 30 seconds later, not sooner, because
leaving restarts the full timeout rather than the remainder.

Proves FR-9.

** 26. Zero waits indefinitely

Set the timeout to 0, which the page explains as waiting indefinitely.
Trigger the bar and leave it ten minutes or more. It is still there, still
answerable, and Yes still closes the duplicates.

Proves FR-9.

** 27. A question left at timeout 0 does not gag the extension

With the timeout still 0, leave a bar unanswered and wait an hour. Navigate
a different tab to a page that has a duplicate open: that tab must be asked.
Go back to the first bar and click Yes: its duplicates must still close if
they still show that page.

Proves FR-10 together with the clause that lets a zero-timeout record
outlive the one-hour cutoff every other question gets.

** 28. Only one question exists at a time

With a bar open in one tab, navigate another tab to a page that has
duplicates. No second bar appears. Answer the first, then navigate that
other tab to the same page again: now it asks.

Proves FR-10.

** 29. Navigating away from a bar does not gag the extension either

Trigger a bar, then navigate that same tab somewhere else without answering.
Now trigger a duplicate in any tab: it must be asked, rather than staying
silent until the abandoned question expires.

Proves FR-10 and the dead-question sweep.

* The settings page

** 30. An Always answered in a banner appears on an open settings page

Open DejaTab's settings in one tab and leave it visible. In another window,
trigger a bar and answer Always. The per-site entry appears on the settings
page without reloading it.

Proves task 6.10.

** 31. An Always answered while the settings page is still loading also appears

Trigger a bar in one window, then open DejaTab's settings and answer Always
within the first two seconds, while the page is still drawing. The entry is
there when the page settles. The page registers its storage listener before
its first read for exactly this gap.

Proves task 6.10.

** 32. The tester agrees with what the extension did

Paste the two addresses from 3 into "Try two addresses", A first. It says
"The same page" and shows the shared key. Untick ~utm_source~ in the
tracking parameter list and compare again: it says the parameters differ and
names the tracking rule as the switch that would match them.

Proves FR-24.

** 33. The tester names an excluded site instead of comparing

Add a host to Excluded sites, then paste two addresses on that host into the
tester. No same-or-different verdict: a line saying nothing would happen
because that site is excluded, naming the site, and telling you to remove it
from the list to test rules against it.

Proves FR-15 and FR-16 as the tester reports them.

** 34. The tester rejects an unreadable address by side

Type ~not a url~ in A and a real address in B. It names address A
specifically and tells you to include the scheme.

Proves FR-24 and the Error Handling row for an address that will not parse.

** 35. A per-site entry overrides only what it names

Add a per-site entry, set one rule to On and leave the rest on Inherit. Its
summary shows one chip for that rule and one saying everything else is
inherited. Change the matching global rule: the per-site value must not
follow it, while a rule left on Inherit must.

Proves FR-17 and FR-18.

** 36. The most specific entry wins

Add entries for ~example.com~ and ~mail.example.com~ with different rules,
then navigate to ~mail.example.com~. The subdomain entry's rules decide,
with no blending of the two.

Proves FR-19 and FR-20.

* The sweep

** 37. It counts, asks once, and closes only on agreement

Open a dozen tabs with several duplicate sets among them, then click "Clean
up open tabs now". One question appears naming both totals, for example
"Close 7 tabs across 3 pages?", offering a confirm and a Cancel and nothing
else: there is no Always or Never here, because a sweep spans many hosts and
neither answer would mean anything. Press Cancel: nothing closes. Click the
button again and confirm: the tabs close, the page reports how many, and one
notification follows.

Proves task 6.9 and FR-25.

** 38. It pairs www with the apex

Switch on "Ignore a leading www." Open ~https://www.example.com/~ and
~https://example.com/~ in two tabs. Run the sweep: it must count 1 tab
across 1 page.

Bucketing tabs by hostname before comparing used to hide exactly this pair,
so this is the check that the sweep applies the same rules as the rest of the
extension.

Proves FR-25.

** 39. It keeps the tab you are looking at

With duplicates open, start the sweep from the settings page: that page is
the active tab and is in no duplicate group, so the most recently accessed
tab in each group survives. Now put the settings page in a second window,
focus a duplicate in the first window, and sweep again: the focused duplicate
must be the one that survives.

Proves the TSD's resolution of its third open question.

** 40. A stale count refuses to close anything

Run the sweep to get a count. Before confirming, close one of the tabs it
counted and reload the settings page. Now confirm: you get a message that the
count has expired and to count the duplicates again, and nothing closes. The
token behind the count is single use, so a confirmation can only ever close
the set the user saw a number for.

* Settings, storage and sync

** 41. Every change takes effect with no restart

Toggle a rule, then immediately create a duplicate that only that rule
matches. The new rule decides, with nothing reloaded and no browser restart.

Proves FR-26.

** 42. Reset clears the recorded hosts too

With entries in both lists, click "Reset to defaults" and confirm. Every rule
goes back to its shipped value, the timeout back to 30 seconds, and both
lists empty, including every host that Always and Never recorded.

Proves FR-27.

** 43. A write that cannot be saved says so

Fill the sync quota by pasting a very long list of names into "Parameters to
drop", or answer Always with sync unavailable. On the settings page you get a
plain message naming the cause and saying the change is in effect until you
close Chrome but was not stored. For an Always or Never answered in a banner,
where the bar has already removed itself and the settings page is probably
closed, you get a notification titled "DejaTab could not save that setting"
instead.

Proves the Error Handling row for a failed write.

** 44. Settings survive a restart and reach a second machine

Configure several rules and both lists, quit Chrome and reopen it:
everything as you left it. Then sign in to the same Chrome profile on a
second machine with the extension installed and open its settings: the same
rules and the same lists.

Proves FR-28.

** 45. A write from another machine appears live

With the settings page open on machine A, change a rule on machine B.
Machine A's page updates itself with no reload. This is the only check that
exercises the page's storage listener with a write that neither this page nor
this worker made.

Proves FR-26 and task 6.10.

* Performance

Three of the four measurements task 7.9 asks for are honestly answerable with
Chrome's own tools. The fourth, comparing page-load timings against a profile
without the extension, is not, and 47 says why rather than inventing a
procedure for it.

** Getting to roughly two hundred tabs

Do not use ~chrome://~ or ~data:~ tabs: the extension rejects those by scheme
before it builds a key, so a profile full of them measures nothing. Serve two
hundred trivial local pages over http instead, which are real candidates and
do get keyed:

#+BEGIN_SRC sh
mkdir -p /tmp/dejatab-load && cd /tmp/dejatab-load
for i in $(seq 1 200); do
  printf '<!doctype html><title>p%s</title><p>%s\n' "$i" "$i" > "p$i.html"
done
python3 -m http.server 8000 &
google-chrome $(for i in $(seq 1 200); do
  printf 'http://localhost:8000/p%s.html ' "$i"
done)
#+END_SRC

Every page is a few bytes, so all two hundred finish loading, which matters
because a tab still loading is filtered out before the extension counts it and
a profile of half-loaded tabs understates the work. Make about twenty of them
duplicates by repeating some numbers in that last command. Kill the server and
the directory afterwards.

** 46. Nothing runs while you read

Open Chrome's Task Manager: Shift+Esc on Windows and Linux, or the Chrome
menu, More tools, Task manager on any platform, which is the only route on
macOS. Navigate somewhere so the extension wakes, and watch for a row reading
"Service worker: DejaTab". It should appear for a moment and disappear within
about thirty seconds. Now read a page for a full minute without navigating:
there must be no DejaTab row at all. A row that never goes away means
something is keeping the worker alive, which is the failure this check exists
to catch.

Proves FR-32 and the PRD's fourth goal.

** 47. Nothing runs on the page's own thread

Open DevTools on an ordinary page, go to the Performance panel, and record a
reload with the extension loaded. Search the main thread's flame chart for
anything attributable to DejaTab: a content script appears there by name.
There must be nothing, because no content script is declared and
~manifest.json~ has no ~content_scripts~ key at all. Repeat with the
extension disabled to confirm the two recordings look the same in shape.

The honest limit, and the reason the PRD's fifth success metric is now
written as presence rather than timings: this is a presence-or-absence check,
not a timing comparison. Run-to-run variance in a page load is far larger
than any effect an extension that declares no content script could have, so
two recordings will differ by tens of milliseconds either way and the
difference will mean nothing. The absence of a DejaTab entry on the page's
thread is the real evidence.

Proves FR-31 and FR-33 as far as they can be proved.

** 48. A navigation stays inside the five-millisecond budget

There is no benchmark harness and none is wanted, so this needs two temporary
lines in the worker. In ~handleNavigation~, capture ~performance.now()~
immediately before the candidate listing and log the delta immediately after
the duplicate selection.

Reload the extension, then open the worker's console: ~chrome://extensions~,
Details on the DejaTab card, then Inspect views: service worker. With two
hundred tabs open, navigate to a page that has many duplicates among them and
read the number. It must be under five milliseconds. Navigate a dozen times
and take the worst reading, not the best. Note the first navigation after the
worker has been stopped separately, because it also pays the worker's own
startup cost, and letting that hide inside an average is how a real
regression stays invisible.

Those two lines are instrumentation, not a change to keep. Remove them before
anything is committed and re-run ~node --test~ to confirm the file is clean.
The extension ships with no logging on the navigation path at all: a console
line per navigation is itself work the budget exists to exclude, so leaving
the instrument in would quietly spend the thing being measured. The only
~console~ calls that stay are on failure paths that run once and rarely: a tab
listing that failed, a notification that could not be shown, a banner
injection Chrome refused.

Proves the TSD's performance budget.

** 49. A single-page application wakes nothing

On ~chrome://extensions~, wait until the DejaTab card reads "service worker
(inactive)", about thirty seconds after the last event. Open the Task Manager
as in 46 and keep it visible. Now open a single-page application, Gmail or any
app that changes its address bar without loading a page, and move between its
views for a full minute. No DejaTab row may appear at any point, and the card
must still read inactive. Trust the row rather than the card's text: the
inactive label does not always refresh on its own, while the Task Manager is
live.

This is the check that the navigation event was chosen correctly, since the
event the worker listens for does not fire for an address a page changes by
itself.

Proves FR-34.

* One question for Chrome itself

** 50. Does Chrome's URL parser keep a host's trailing root dot?

In DevTools on any page, evaluate ~new URL("https://example.com./a").hostname~.
Node 25 returns ~"example.com."~, which is why both modules strip that dot. If
Chrome returns ~"example.com"~ instead, the stripping stays and is harmless,
but the comment in the settings module asserting that Chrome keeps the dot
overstates the case and should be reworded.

Item 16 is the behavioural half of this and matters whichever way the answer
goes. This item is only about whether that comment is true.
